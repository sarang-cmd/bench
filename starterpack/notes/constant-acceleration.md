# Motion with constant acceleration

Write down five symbols and the rest is algebra.

- `s` displacement in metres
- `u` starting velocity in m/s
- `v` velocity at the end in m/s
- `a` acceleration in m/s squared
- `t` time in seconds

## The four equations

```
v = u + a t
s = u t + a t t / 2
v v = u u + 2 a s
s = (u + v) t / 2
```

Each one leaves out exactly one symbol, which is why a question tells you which
one to use: pick the equation without the quantity you do not have and do not
want.

## Choosing

> A ball is dropped, how far has it fallen after 2 s?

You have `u = 0`, `a = 9.81`, `t = 2` and want `s`. The second equation has all
four, so use it.

## Habits that save marks

1. Write the symbols with their values before touching the calculator.
2. Keep the sign of `a`: upwards positive means a falling object has a negative
   acceleration.
3. Give the answer to the same number of significant figures as the least
   precise value in the question.
4. Add the unit on the same line as the number, not three lines later.

## Where it stops being true

The equations only hold while the acceleration is constant. A parachute opening,
a rocket burning out or a collision all break that, and then you work in stages
or with a graph.
