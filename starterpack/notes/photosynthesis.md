# Photosynthesis, in one page

A plant turns light, water and carbon dioxide into sugar and oxygen. Everything
else in this note is a detail of that sentence.

## The two stages

- **Light reactions** happen in the thylakoid membranes. Light excites
  electrons in chlorophyll, water is split, oxygen leaves and the energy is
  carried away as ATP and reduced NADP.
- **The Calvin cycle** happens in the stroma and needs no light directly. It
  fixes carbon dioxide onto a five-carbon sugar, and uses the ATP and NADP from
  the first stage to build triose phosphate.

## What limits the rate

Three things can be in short supply at once:

1. light intensity
2. carbon dioxide concentration
3. temperature, because the enzymes have an optimum

The slowest of the three sets the pace. That is what "limiting factor" means.

## Numbers worth remembering

- The pigments absorb best around 430 nm and 660 nm.
- Most crop plants peak near 25 C; the enzymes lose shape above that.

> If a graph of rate against light intensity flattens out, light is no longer
> limiting. Something else is.

## The summary equation

```
6 CO2 + 6 H2O  ->  C6H12O6 + 6 O2
```

The six water molecules on the left are split for their electrons, not for the
oxygen that ends up in the sugar.

## A sentence to write in an exam

Light energy is converted to chemical energy in the thylakoids; the Calvin cycle
uses that energy to reduce carbon dioxide to carbohydrate.
